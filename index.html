All county averages and method

We sample a 24 × 24 grid across each county’s bounding box, retain points within its boundaries, and average each point’s distance to its nearest provider anywhere in the saved California dataset. Latitude weights approximate land-area weighting; these are not population-weighted distances. Small islands may be missed. The synthetic map treats each county circle with a nonzero provider count as one fictional hub; its providers are assumed to share that location.

AI visualization · Design rationale

Why the maps are shown this way

This visualization is designed to explore how adolescent birth rates and provider distribution relate geographically. The observed map uses the saved data; the AI-created scenario demonstrates a way to compare patterns using invented values. It does not identify where providers should be placed.

Color shows rates; circles show providers

County shading makes differences in birth rates visible across California. Both maps use the same color thresholds so the same shade always means the same rate range. On the observed map, each dot is a listed AFLP address. On the synthetic map, circle area represents an invented provider count, letting viewers compare rates and counts without combining them into an unexplained score.

The fictional pattern is a demonstration

Synthetic rates and provider counts were assigned independently using a repeatable county-name-based method. They were not inferred from a county’s residents, health outcomes, or actual services. A dark county with a small circle illustrates what higher rates alongside fewer providers could look like; it is not evidence that this county is underserved.

Distance adds a different perspective

A provider count alone does not show how far someone may need to travel. The hover box measures distance from the selected point to the nearest mapped provider, including across county lines. The county average samples locations throughout the county rather than measuring only from its center. This describes geographic proximity, not the average experience of residents.

The assumptions matter

Hover times assume straight-line travel at 30 mph, so they are illustrative rather than driving estimates. Synthetic circles act as fictional hubs, with all providers in a county assumed to share one location. Actual access also depends on roads, transportation, cost, eligibility, and provider capacity—information these maps do not contain.

How to interpret the comparison: Use it to ask where closer investigation might be useful. Higher birth rates, fewer providers, or longer distances can suggest questions to explore, but these maps alone cannot establish unmet need or show that provider availability causes birth rates to change.

Data sources

  • CDPH — Adolescent Births. Supplied CSV; 2024 single-year “Adolescent Birth Rate,” ages 15–19, total population. Original data · CDPH methodology.
  • CDPH — Adolescent Family Life Program, Where We Are. Public directory retrieved September 5, 2026; 17 addresses matched by the US Census Bureau and one street-level approximation from OpenStreetMap. Directory snapshot.
  • California Department of Technology — California County Boundaries and Identifiers. Without coastal buffers; retrieved September 5, 2026 and simplified for display.

Limitations

Adolescent birth data represent live births and do not represent every pregnant youth. Publicly listed AFLP locations indicate where programs are located, but do not directly measure actual service accessibility or capacity. The directory snapshot and birth data describe different dates; this map does not establish causal relationships.

Following CDPH’s suppression rule, rates and birth counts with a numerator below 10 are shown as “No data,” including zero-coded rows in the supplied export. This dashboard uses the latest single year available in that file; CDPH’s own county dashboard uses three-year aggregates. Geocoded mailing addresses are approximate and may differ from service delivery sites. The Madera point is a street-level approximation on Sunrise Avenue, not a verified building location.
